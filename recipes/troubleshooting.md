# Pour-over troubleshooting

## Method

Change exactly one variable per brew, in priority order: grind, then temperature,
then ratio, then agitation and pour technique, then bloom. Grind moves extraction
the most per step; ratio changes strength more than extraction; technique fixes
uniformity. Changing two things at once means the next cup teaches you nothing.

## Sour or thin cups

Sourness with a thin body is underextraction. Grind one step finer. If the drawdown
was already slow or stalled, grind finer will worsen it — raise water temperature
2-3°C instead (stop at 96°C). If temperature is maxed too, lengthen the bloom and
wet the grounds evenly with a gentle swirl.

## Bitter or astringent cups

Bitterness with a drying, sandpaper finish is overextraction, usually with fines
migration. Grind one step coarser. If the grind is already coarse, drop the water
temperature 2-3°C (not below about 88°C). Astringency specifically tracks agitation:
pour gently and skip late stirring.

## Weak but balanced cups

If nothing tastes wrong but the cup is watery, extraction is fine and the recipe is
just dilute. Use about one gram more coffee (move from 1:16.5 toward 1:15). Do not
grind finer first — that changes extraction and risks trading weakness for bitterness.

## Heavy, muddy cups

Too concentrated: add water (toward 1:16.5-1:17) or grind slightly coarser.

## Hollow or muted cups

Decent acidity but missing sweetness and mid-palate usually means uneven extraction —
some grounds over-, some under-extracted. Fix the bed: even spiral pours, a gentle
swirl after the final pour, and a flat bed at the end of the brew tell you the water
passed through evenly.

## Stalled drawdowns

A drawdown far past the method's target means fines are clogging the filter. Grind
coarser and pour more gently with fewer pulses. If a burr grinder suddenly produces
more fines at the same setting, check burr seating and alignment.

## Fast drawdowns

Water rushing through in well under the target time means the grind is too coarse or
the water is channeling. Grind finer and use more, smaller pours to keep the water
level low and even.
