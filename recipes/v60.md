# Hario V60 notes

## Baseline recipe

15-16g coffee to 250g water (about 1:15.5 to 1:16), water at 92-96°C depending on
roast. Bloom with 2-3x the coffee weight in water for 30-45 seconds, then pour in
two or three even spiral pulses. Target total drawdown between 2:30 and 4:00 for
this dose; much past 4:00 means the bed is clogging.

## Grind

Medium-fine, similar to table salt. Grind is the highest-leverage variable on the
V60: one step finer meaningfully raises extraction and slows the drawdown; one step
coarser does the opposite. Change grind before touching anything else, unless the
drawdown is already slow or stalled — then a finer grind will make the stall worse.

## Temperature

Light roasts want hotter water (94-96°C); darker roasts extract easily and prefer
88-92°C. Raising temperature increases extraction without adding fines, which makes
it the right lever when a brew is sour but the drawdown is already on the slow side.
Stay at or below 96°C: beyond that you gain little and risk harshness.

## Drawdown

A fast drawdown (under 2:30) usually means the grind is too coarse or water is
channeling past the bed; the cup comes out sour or weak. A slow or stalled drawdown
(past 4:00-5:00) means excess fines are packing the filter — grind coarser and pour
more gently rather than grinding finer. Note the drawdown time every brew; it is the
single most diagnostic number you can log.

## Common defects

Sour and thin means underextraction: grind finer first, or raise temperature if the
drawdown is already slow. Bitter with a drying, astringent finish means
overextraction and fines: grind coarser, then reduce agitation. Weak but otherwise
balanced is a strength problem, not extraction — use more coffee (lower the ratio
toward 1:15) instead of changing the grind. A hollow, muted cup with reasonable
acidity usually points to channeling: even out the pour and add a gentle swirl after
the last pulse.
